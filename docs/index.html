Why this matters now

Working with AI is mostly writing

Prompts, context, specs, corrections, the long explanation of what you actually meant. Every agent, every chat, every code review comment is you producing text, all day.

Nobody budgets for that. It is the quiet tax on working this way, and it is paid in the slowest input method you own. Here is what one month of it measures, taken from a real log rather than a projection.

Typing it at 40 words per minute, an average pace 104 hours
Saying it at 145 words per minute, the pace measured in that log 29 hours

That is 75 hours back in 36 days. Nearly two full working weeks, recovered from a month of ordinary work, by saying things instead of typing them.

The part that matters more

Cheap input changes what you tell the machine

Speed is the obvious benefit. It is not the interesting one.

When typing is the bottleneck, you compress. You give the agent the summary you could be bothered to type, not the full picture, and then you spend three more rounds correcting an answer built on the half you left out.

When talking is instant, free and unmetered, that instinct disappears. You explain the whole situation, the constraint you have not mentioned yet, the thing you tried on Tuesday that failed. The agent gets what it needed the first time. Better context in, better work out, and no meter running to make you think twice.

Using it

One key, two ways to hold it

There is no window to open and no button to find. Loqui lives in the menu bar and waits for the shortcut you choose.

Hold

Push to talk

Hold the key, say the thing, let go. The text is pasted the moment you release, like a walkie talkie.

Tap

Or go hands free

Tap once and keep talking for as long as you need. Tap again when you are done and it lands.

Done

It appears where you are

Straight into whatever has your cursor. Terminal, browser, editor, chat box. It is a keystroke, not an integration.

What you get

Small on purpose, sharp where it counts

Nine things, grouped by the part of the job they do. Each one is here because it removes a step you would otherwise do yourself.

Getting the words in

The capture has to be effortless or you go back to typing.

Two modes, one key

Hold for a quick thought, tap for a long one. Same shortcut, and you can set it to whatever your hands already reach for.

Talk as long as you need

No arbitrary cut off at thirty seconds. The longest single dictation in that log ran to 951 words, which is roughly a full page of specification, spoken once.

Everywhere, not somewhere

It types into the frontmost app, so it works in your terminal, your agent, your editor and your email without any of them knowing it exists.

What comes out

The difference between a transcript and something you can actually send.

Tidied as you speak

Filler words removed, capitals and full stops restored. What lands is text you can send, not a wall of lowercase you have to repair first.

Your vocabulary, learned

Client names, product names, the acronyms your industry insists on. Add them to the dictionary once and Loqui stops mangling them.

Never drops your words

It pastes at the cursor. If there is nowhere to paste, it goes to the clipboard instead of disappearing, so a mis-aimed dictation is never lost.

Living with it

It runs all day, so it had better be quiet and honest.

One glyph, no window

A microphone in the menu bar that pulses while it listens. No dock icon, nothing to close, nothing in your way.

History on your terms

Everything you dictate, searchable, with a Recent menu for grabbing it again. Keep it forever, keep a week, or switch it off entirely.

Proof, not vibes

The stats window tracks words, pace and hours saved, so you can decide for yourself whether it is worth the keystroke.

Privacy

Your voice never leaves your Mac

Worth being specific about, because every claim here can be checked against the source rather than taken on trust.

  • ✓ Transcription happens on device. Loqui uses the speech engine already built into macOS. No audio is uploaded, because there is no server to upload it to.
  • ✓ One network request exists. Checking this page for a newer version. That is the only connection in the codebase, and you can grep for it yourself.
  • ✓ No analytics, telemetry or crash reporting. Not anonymised, not aggregated, not opt out. There is none.
  • ✓ Your history stays on your disk. In your own Application Support folder, with retention you control, including off. Deleting it in the app deletes it from disk.
  • ✓ Logs never contain your words. Diagnostics record events and word counts, never what you said.

The idea

Why it exists

macOS already ships a speech engine that runs on the machine. A global shortcut is not hard. Pasting at the cursor is a solved problem. None of that adds up to a monthly subscription, an account to create, or a good reason to send your voice to a server so it can come back as text.

Loqui is what is left once you take all of that away. It is faster because nothing leaves the machine. It costs nothing to run because there is no service behind it. And because there is no meter, there is no reason to ration how much you say, which turns out to matter more than the speed.

It is open source so you can read every line, check the privacy claims for yourself, and build it in one command if you would rather not trust a download.

Get it

Install it

Signed and notarised by Apple, so it opens without the right click routine.

Download the latest release

Or build it yourself. No Apple developer account needed, just Xcode.

git clone https://github.com/jameslaws/loqui.git
cd loqui
./install.sh

macOS asks for two permissions on first use. Accessibility lets Loqui notice your shortcut anywhere and paste at the cursor. Microphone lets it hear you. Both are required, and neither sends anything anywhere.
